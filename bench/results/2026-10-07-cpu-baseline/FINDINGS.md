# CPU tier, Phase 0: baseline and stakes

Protocol: `bench/cpu/PROTOCOL.md` (pre-registered at `b40a1dd`, Amendment 1
after rep 0). Tables: `report.md` (generated by `bench/cpu/report.py`). Raw
rows: `raw.jsonl` (campaign), `stakes.jsonl` (offline stakes),
`ea_profile.jsonl` (efficient-apriori profile).

## Summary

- **Baseline.** The CPU route already beats efficient-apriori (EA) on deepk
  and skew, 7.5–10× at T1 and 23–28× at T4, and on smoke at T4. It ties smoke
  at T1. It loses wide, or005, or003 and or002 by 1.04–3.6× at T1 and T4, and
  or0001k2 by 5–10×.
- **Where the time goes.** Five costs explain almost all of the losing time:
  - K=2 counts every candidate pair one at a time: 22–211 s at T1, and
    2× worse with `n_jobs=4` on the sparse route.
  - The boolean matrix build: 8–32 s at T1.
  - K≥3 candidate generation is quadratic when every prefix group has fewer
    than 100 members: 391–406 s on or002, of which K=6 alone is 354 s.
  - K≥3 counting goes per candidate: 74–104 s on or002.
  - Per-level overheads and the emit step: 45 s on or0001k2.
- **Stakes.** Each of the five levers reproduces the exact lattice on all eight
  workloads: same itemset hash and count sum as the campaign, every level's
  candidate and frequent counts asserted. Composed, they project to **0.02–0.11×
  EA's time in every regime at both thread settings**: from 0.54 s (or005) to
  9.9 s (or002, against EA's 167 s).
- **Recommendation.** GO for all five. Build order L2 → L1 → L4 → L3 → L5.
  Three decisions are the owner's, listed at the end; the first is whether the
  Polars boolean-matrix counter goes.

- **K=2.** The sparse route's K=2 is one scipy product (0.3–1.0 s) followed by
  2.76M–10.4M scalar `cooccur[i, j]` look-ups. The Polars route builds one
  expression per pair; of its 74.6 s on wide, 53.9 s is the `collect`.
  - With `n_jobs=4` the sparse route takes the threaded chunk path, and K=2
    gets slower: wide 62.7 → 130.6 s, or003 45.4 → 100.3 s, or002
    67.5 → 148.1 s, or0001k2 207.6 → 463 s. That is why F-auto and F-sparse are
    slower at T4 than at T1 on every Online Retail regime and on wide.
- **Build.** One `list.contains` per frequent item: 28.9 s for 2,351 items on
  wide at T1 (7.4 s at T4).
- **K≥3 generation.** or002 K=6 takes 354 s for 31,810 candidates from 73,597
  5-itemsets, and K=7 takes 23.6 s. Every prefix group has fewer than 100
  members, so `_generate_candidates_vectorized` falls back to the pairwise loop
  over all 73,597 itemsets. The run that hit its cap (`or002-F-polars-T1`) spent
  371 s at that level alone. or003 K=5 is the same case at 8.3 s.
- **K≥3 counting.** Rust takes 69 s at T1 and 19 s at T4 on or002. Polars
  expressions take 102 s at T4. The sparse route also converts the Polars matrix
  to CSR again at every level: 5.2 s on deepk, 5.3 s on skew.
- **Overhead and emit.**
  - or0001k2: `_build_result_df` 25.8 s for 2.68M itemsets, plus the K=2
    threshold loop over a 10.4M-entry dict, 18.4 s.
  - deepk: the length filter (`sum_horizontal` + filtered copy), 1.4 s over 11
    levels.
- **EA** (finding 6). Counting is nearly all of EA's time, and nearly all of
  counting is `set.intersection`:

### What each stake showed

- **L1 (K=2 from one Gram matrix).**
  - `gram_sparse` (scipy `M.T @ M`, upper triangle ≥ min_count) and
    `gram_dense` take 0.02–1.9 s. scipy's product is single-threaded.
  - The Polars self-join (`polars_pairs`, the counter a Polars-only route would
    use) takes 1.3–5.9 s at T1 and 0.4–1.9 s at T4 (smoke: 0.04 / 0.02 s). It
    materialises one row per co-occurring pair occurrence: 25–62M rows, up to
    1.2 GB RSS.
  - The dense Gram costs n_items² × 4 B: 83 MB on or0001k2 (4,565 items),
    4.9 GB at stress_k2's 35K items. It needs a budget, with column-blocked
    products above it.
- **L2 (CSR from the list column).**
  - `numpy_map` (Polars explode + `np.searchsorted`) and `explode_join` (the
    GPU route's `_build_csr_from_transactions`) take 0.04–2.8 s; neither wins
    everywhere.
  - The stake allocates int64 temporaries: +323–523 MB RSS on deepk and skew.
    The 25 % rule there allows about +75–83 MB over F-auto's 299–331 MB. Phase 1
    needs an int32, row-chunked build (budget below).
- **L4 (array candidate generation).** It matches `_generate_candidates`'
  candidate list exactly at every level: asserted where it ran, and by the
  campaign's per-level candidate counts on or002. It takes 0.0006–4.2 s per
  level.
  - The 4.2 s is or002 K=3: 2.6M candidates, subset test by binary search on
    packed keys.
  - At K=3 the only subset test is "is (a, b) a frequent pair". The K=2 Gram
    mask answers that in O(1) per candidate.
- **L3 (K≥3 counting).**
  - `proj` (one scipy Gram per prefix group, over the prefix's rows) wins large
    groups. or002 K=3 (2.6M candidates in 1,231 groups): proj 2.0 s, gbitvec
    11.9 s, per-candidate bitvec 21.6 s, Rust 49.8 s at T1 (13.3 s at T4).
  - `gbitvec` (prefix AND once, then pair popcounts on the prefix's non-zero
    words) wins small groups: or002 K=4 1.27 s against proj 7.15 s; deepk and
    skew at every level.
  - Crossover per group: at ≤ 100K rows (570–1,563 words) gbitvec is faster up
    to 33–64 suffixes, proj from 65–128 suffixes. At 1M rows (15,625 words)
    gbitvec is faster in every measured bucket up to 128 suffixes. proj carries
    about 400 µs of fixed cost per group, gbitvec about 36 µs.
  - Rust `count_itemsets_simd` never wins a level at T1. At T4 it wins one level
    against the best single-threaded numpy variant: skew K=3, 0.59 vs 0.83 s
    (0.24 s, under rule 2's 1 s).
- **L5 (overheads).**
  - The length filter on CSR row lengths, the int64 casts and a threshold mask
    on a count array cost milliseconds.
  - Emitting the lattice from per-level arrays (`bench/cpu/stake_emit.py`, the
    campaign's level sizes) takes 0.20 s for or0001k2's 2.68M itemsets, against
    25.8 s for `_build_result_df` plus 18.4 s for the dict threshold loop.
- **Reference: the existing `apriori_from_csr` (Rust).**
  - Faster than the composed projection on deepk (1.12 / 0.40 s) and skew
    (1.31 / 0.48 s): its bitvector K≥3 counting is multi-threaded and builds
    its bitvectors in Rust.
  - Slower on Online Retail: or002 76.7 / 68.0 s (its K≥3 generation probes a
    `HashSet<Vec<usize>>` per subset), or0001k2 7.7 / 3.2 s.

### Memory budgets for Phase 1 (each with a tested fallback)

| structure | size | budget | fallback |
|---|---|---|---|
| CSR (int32 indices, int64 indptr) | 4 B × nnz + 8 B × rows: deepk 40 MB, or0001k2 4 MB | always | built in row chunks of ≤ 8M non-zeros, so temporaries stay ≤ 64 MB |
| Dense K=2 Gram | 4 B × n² | ≤ 256 MB (n ≤ 8,192) | sparse product plus upper-triangle filter; above 1 GB of sparse output, column blocks of the product |
| Column bitvectors | n_items × ⌈rows/64⌉ × 8 B: deepk 14 MB, wide 29 MB | ≤ 512 MB | the proj/CSR counter for every group |
| Level arrays | (n, k) int32 + int64 counts | the level's itemsets | — (smaller than today's tuples and dicts) |

## Findings

1. **(must-fix) K≥3 candidate generation is quadratic** when every prefix group
   has fewer than 100 members (`_generate_candidates_vectorized` falls back to
   `_generate_candidates_simple`, which compares all pairs and never breaks on a
   prefix change). or002 K=6: 354 s for 31,810 candidates; or002 K≥3 generation
   391–406 s of 559–595 s; or003 K=5: 8.3 s. L4: 4.72 s for all of or002.
2. **(must-fix) K=2 counts candidates one at a time on both routes**: 22–211 s
   at T1 (wide, Online Retail), plus a materialised list of C(n,2) string
   tuples. L1: 0.02–1.9 s.
3. **(must-fix) `n_jobs > 1` makes the sparse K=2 path about 2× slower**:
   threaded chunks of GIL-bound scalar look-ups, with the columns re-extracted
   per chunk. wide 62.7 → 130.6 s; or0001k2 207.6 → 463 s. F-auto at T4 is
   slower than at T1 on every Online Retail regime and on wide. L1 removes the
   path.
4. **(should) The boolean matrix build runs one `list.contains` per frequent
   item**: 7.4–28.9 s at T1, 2–7.5 s at T4. L2: 0.03–2.8 s. Polars 2.0 check
   (asked for): the old streaming undercount does not reproduce on 1.43.2 or
   2.0.0 (`bench/cpu/list_contains_check.py`, 600 items up to 2M rows). The
   build now names the in-memory engine, which is faster for this query.
5. **(should) K≥3 counting is per candidate**: Polars `all_horizontal` per
   candidate, or Rust SIMD per candidate after a CSR rebuild per level. or002
   78 s at T1. L3 with a group-size dispatch: 4.6 s at T1.
6. **(should) Per-level overheads**: CSR rebuilt from the Polars matrix every
   level (deepk 7 s), length filter (deepk 1.4 s), dict threshold loop and
   tuple emission (or0001k2 44 s). L5: ≤ 0.2 s.
7. **(should) Memory**: the CPU route holds 1.9–2.1× EA's peak RSS on wide and
   Online Retail (or0001k2 5.0 vs 2.4 GB): C(n,2) tuples and tuple-keyed dicts.
   The array levers remove both. The CSR build must stay int32 and row-chunked
   to respect the 25 % rule on deepk and skew.
8. **(should) EA's earlier timing included non-mining work.**
   `efficient_apriori.apriori()` calls the miner with
   `output_transaction_ids=True` and then builds rules. On or0001k2: 46.0 s
   plain, 105.3 s with row ids, +5.0 s rules. The 2026-10-01 baseline (87 s on
   a Ryzen 4600G) timed that call; this campaign times
   `itemsets_from_transactions` (50.7 s here).
9. **(nice) Finding 6 confirmed**: EA is a vertical tidset miner and spends
   88–99 % of its counting time in `set.intersection`.
10. **(nice) The Rust role R1 (CPU K>2 counting) would disappear** with L3: Rust
    `count_itemsets_simd` wins one of 40 K≥3 levels (skew K=3 at T4, by 0.24 s). The
    exported `apriori_from_csr` is a public API and is not affected.
    `bench/consolidation` rule 7 then applies to R1.
11. **(nice) `tests/test_smoke_correctness.py::test_support_001`** (Online Retail
    at 0.001 on the CPU route) is deselected in every gate because it ran
    > 10 min at 9+ GB here. It compares only row counts with EA, without the
    oracle convention. After Phase 1 it may fit the gate.
12. **(nice) Harness caveat**: EA rows' `cpu_s` includes building the result
    frame after the timer (or0001k2: 71 s CPU for 50.7 s wall); wall times are
    unaffected.
13. **(nice) Out of scope, logged**: ECLAT and FP-growth as public miners;
    multi-threaded numpy counting (prefix groups over a thread pool) is a Phase
    1 measurement, not a lever here.

## Recommended build order and go/no-go

Go = the projection meets rule 2 against the current baseline in at least one
regime, within the memory budget above.

| order | lever | go? | best regime (now → projected, rule 2) | depends on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **L2** CSR from the list column, once per run (int32, row-chunked; reuse `_build_csr_from_transactions` where its memory fits) | GO | wide T1 31.7 → 0.13 s | — |
| 2 | **L1** K=2 from one Gram matrix (dense ≤ 256 MB, else sparse or column-blocked); no C(n,2) list | GO | or0001k2 T4 463 → 1.2 s | L2 |
| 3 | **L4** K≥3 generation on sorted int32 arrays (prefix runs, subset test by packed-key binary search; K=3 from the Gram mask) | GO | or002 T1 412 → 4.7 s | L1 (arrays) |
| 4 | **L3** K≥3 counting: gbitvec, and proj for groups above a measured suffix count per word count (rule 4) | GO | or002 T1 76 → 4.6 s | L2, L4 |
| 5 | **L5** array levels end to end: length filter from CSR row lengths, no dict merge, counts as arrays, emission from arrays; Pascal inference and free-sets ported to arrays | GO | or0001k2 T1 45 → 0.2 s | L4 |

- **Why this order.** L2 is the representation every later lever counts on. L1
  is the largest saving, on every losing regime. L4 and L3 together take or002
  from 559–595 s to about 10 s. L5 finishes the array path and carries the
  free-set and Pascal ports.
- **Dispatch.**
  - `_choose_counting_strategy` and the 1,000 / 100 thresholds in
    `_generate_candidates` disappear: one engine on every regime (rule 5).
  - K=2's dense/sparse/blocked choice uses n_items against the budget.
  - L3's proj/gbitvec choice uses the suffixes per prefix group and the word
    count. A sweep like `bench/group_crossover.py` pins the crossover first.
- **Shared code stays.** SON keeps `build_boolean_matrix`,
  `count_support_batched` and `_generate_candidates`. The exported counters keep
  their behaviour. The GPU route's `_build_csr_from_transactions` is reused,
  not changed.

## Decisions for the owner

1. **The Polars boolean-matrix counter.** The stakes say one array engine should
   serve `sparse=False` and `sparse=None` alike. A Polars-only K=2 (self-join)
   is 2–10× slower than the Gram and uses 0.6–1.2 GB. The Polars K≥3 counter
   cannot beat EA at T1 on or002 even after L4 (102 s at T4 now). Proposal:
   remove the Polars counter from the CPU route, keep Polars as the input layer
   (scan, explode, group_by), and deprecate `sparse`.
2. **NumPy floor.** gbitvec uses `np.bitwise_count` (NumPy ≥ 2.0). The 16-bit
   lookup fallback works on the current `numpy>=1.20` floor and costs up to 2×
   on popcount-heavy levels (per-candidate bitvec or002 K=3: 21.6 vs 44.2 s),
   where proj wins anyway. Proposal: keep the floor, use the fallback below 2.0,
   and measure both in Phase 1.
3. **Repetitions.** wide, or003, or002 and or0001k2 have one baseline rep.
   Two more baseline reps of those four cost about 4 h on this box (7,234 s per
   rep). The Phase 1 campaign itself, at 3 reps, is about 45 min by the
   projection, mostly EA (~31 min). Proposal: keep the one-rep baseline cells
   (the projected gaps are 10–300×) and run the Phase 1 campaign at 3 reps.
