# Route consolidation, round 2: measurement protocol

Fixed before the first timed run. The goal: the fastest exact path per decision, on the GPU and on the CPU;
everything that loses goes. The route map it starts from is `INVENTORY.md` (`main` at `895f3ab`). The
configs are generated by `bench/routes_matrix.py`, run by `bench/routes_run.py` through
`bench/runner.py --mode routes-cpu|routes`, and decided by `bench/routes/decide.py`.

## Box, budgets, pins

- **Box.** 2× RTX 3060 12 GB (sm_86, no peer access, `NCCL_P2P_DISABLE=1` on every row), 2× Xeon E5-2680
  v4 (2 × 14 cores, 56 threads), a cgroup quota of 17.92 CPUs (`cpu.max 1792000 100000`) and 34.8 GiB
  (`memory.max`), driver 580.159.03 (CUDA 13.0), CuPy 14.1.1. Every row records `nproc`, `os.cpu_count()`,
  the CPU model, the cgroup limits and the thread pools. Not the moonshots box (EPYC 7282): no number here
  is compared with one from another box.
- **Threads.** `THREADS = 17` for every pool (`POLARS_MAX_THREADS`, `RAYON_NUM_THREADS`, `OMP_NUM_THREADS`,
  `MKL_NUM_THREADS`, `OPENBLAS_NUM_THREADS`), set in the child's environment before polars or numpy is
  imported, and `n_jobs=17` on the CPU route and SON's CPU passes. The brief's default is the physical
  core count (28); the cgroup quota caps the process at 17.92 CPUs, and 28 busy threads under it are
  throttled by the CFS bandwidth controller, so the quota's floor is the box's usable core count.
- **Budgets.** Phase 2a (CPU) ≤ 8 wall-hours (`--max-hours 8`); Phase 2b (GPU) ≤ 6 GPU-hours, counted as Σ
  (process wall × devices the config uses) (`--max-gpu-hours 6`). The census below is a harness check and
  is not counted. The two phases run one after the other on this box, never together.
- **GPU count.** One GPU means `CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=0`. Two GPUs only for G3, G5 and S2.
- **Scale.** `ET_BENCH_ALPHAFOLD` is unset on this box (no base214m parquet). The scale regime is the new
  preset `deep_sparse_xl` (`dslxl`): `deep_sparse_large`'s shape at 3× its rows (60M rows, ~741M entries;
  ~0.6 GiB of bitvectors, ~3 GiB of device-layer entries), generated in 20M-row blocks;
  `check_preset_purpose` holds it to that.

## Microbenchmarks

- **H.** `bench/microbench_rust.py dump` mines through `apriori(use_gpu=True)` with spies that save every
  R2 call's input (`build_k3plus_groups_from_flat`, not the ESCO variant) and every R4 call's
  (`_prune_non_free_mask`), or with `--son-chunk` every CSR SON's GPU passes hand to
  `_build_gpu_bitvec_matrix` (R5). `time` runs each saved call 3 times: R2 Rust against
  `_build_k3plus_groups_numpy`, R4 Rust (with the fancy-index after it) against the Python prune, R5 the
  Rust build plus its copy to device 0 against the CUDA build it backs (`build_bitvecs_gpu_from_scipy`,
  checked bit-identical). A fallback still running after 300 s is a lower bound. `RAYON_NUM_THREADS=17`.
- **C4.** `bench/routes/count_api.py dump` saves batches of at most 10,000 itemsets (an even stride
  through the level) with the boolean matrix they are counted over: every in-core level K of or002, wide
  and dsl (the CPU route's candidates of K, generated by `cpu_miner.generate_candidates` from the mined
  K−1, and every pair of frequent items at K=2) and or005's SON pass-2 candidate list on chunk 0 (deepk's
  pass 2 counts nothing: the partition bound settles every candidate). `time` runs each arm 3 times per
  batch, each (batch, arm) in a fresh process (a kill, out of memory, is recorded as that arm's failure and the run
  goes on), a call cut at 300 s as a lower bound, and checks the counts equal across the arms: `polars`
  (`count_support_batched(sparse=False, batch_size=10_000)`), `sparse` (`sparse=True, n_jobs=17`),
  `arrays` (the same length filter, then the matrix's CSR counted by `cpu_miner.count_itemsets` with a
  pool of 17).

## Repetitions and order

Fresh process per config. The child compiles every registered kernel on every device a GPU route uses and
runs the same route and arm on a 20,000-row slice of smoke before the timed call (SON: 4 chunks). 3 reps,
rep-major: every config's rep 0 (the one-off axes first: SON on the GPU, ESCO, the cast variants), then
reps 1–2 of the regimes at or above 1 s, then reps 1–2 of the sub-second ones (`routes_matrix.SUBSECOND`),
so a budget stop sheds those first. One rep is allowed for a config over 30 minutes (flagged); a config whose rep 0 timed out or failed is
not repeated (`routes_matrix.rep0_failed_skip`: its reps 1–2 are recorded as skipped); dslxl on
the CPU route runs once (report only). Timeout 900 s per config, 1,800 s for SON and the dslxl CPU row; a
timeout's time is a lower bound. Medians with [min, max].

Rows record: route and arm, `wall_s` (the mining call; `C-bitvecs` excludes the bitvector build,
`bitvec_build_s`; `RUST` includes its CSR, `csr_s`), `cpu_s`, per-level ms and the level split
(`bench/level_split.py`) on the row-split routes, SON's passes (`pass1_s`, `pass2_s` from
`progress_callback`), peak VRAM per device, peak RSS, throttle reasons, the emitted item dtype, the
fallback detector (`consolidation_run.FALLBACK_PATTERNS`, which fails the config), the result signature
(itemsets, summed counts, hash; the `str` and `wide` variants map the items back first) and the tree
digest (`runner._git_rev`). No tracked file is edited and nothing is committed while a timed run runs.

## Decision rule (as given; applied mechanically by `decide.py`)

1. **Signature mismatch or logged fallback.** Either disqualifies that arm. Report it as `(must-fix)` and
   remove nothing on that DP until it is fixed.
2. **Winning a regime.** An arm wins a regime when all three hold:
   - its median is ≥ 10% below the best alternative;
   - the two [min, max] ranges don't overlap;
   - it saves ≥ 1 s absolute.
3. **Dispatch members (G3, G5, C1, C2).** Use leave-one-out.
   - Member X stays when the full dispatch beats "without X" in at least one regime.
   - X goes when "without X" wins somewhere and loses nowhere, or when neither wins anywhere.
   - When both win somewhere, apply rule 5.
4. **Whole paths (G1, G2, G6, G7, C3, C4, S1, S2).** Compare head-to-head. A path that wins no regime goes.
5. **Partial winner.** It stays only with a dispatch rule from facts known before the level runs
   (group-size histogram, candidate count, mean support, free VRAM, dtype). Implement the rule and record
   the measured crossover. Without such a rule, keep the arm with the best geometric-mean time across
   regimes and record what it loses and where.
6. **Ties everywhere.** Keep what serves the most routes with the least code. Break remaining ties on peak
   memory.
7. **Capability guard.** A removal is blocked when, without the arm, any regime fails where it ran (out of
   memory, error, timeout), or when peak VRAM or RSS rises more than 1.25×. Then either port the
   capability (≤ ~150 lines, with tests) or keep the arm as a named fallback, called by its role.
8. **Scale guard.** G1 and G5 removals are decided only after the scale regime ran. Without it, the DP is
   undecided (stop and ask).
9. **SON** is never removed as a strategy.
10. **Rust per role.** A host role goes when its fallback is within 10% per call (median over the dumped
    calls) in every regime where the role runs, or when its path disappears. `apriori_from_csr` follows C3.
11. **Below the floor.** Every regime that a removal slows by ≥ 25%, even under 1 s, is listed as
    `(should)` in FINDINGS. It doesn't change the decision.

## Interpretations fixed here

- A regime is one (workload, min_support, max_length, option set, input variant, GPU count). A config
  that errors or times out cannot win its regime, and a timeout's time is a lower bound.
- "Best alternative" in rule 2 is the fastest other arm of the same DP in that regime. In a leave-one-out
  the two arms are the full dispatch and the dispatch without the member; a member is compared only in
  the regimes where its leave-out arm ran (a regime where the member does not run gives the same work on
  both arms and is not measured twice).
- Where a member is the only kernel for some work (on several GPUs a group beyond one chunk runs
  per-candidate whatever `ET_MINER_LEAVE_OUT` says; the global-atomics row-wise K=2 is the only one for a
  chunk beyond `K2_ROWS_SHARED_PAIRS`), rule 7 keeps it by role whatever the leave-one-out says: the
  leave-out arm cannot remove that work, so a removal would lose it.
- G1's paths are the input layers: the device layer (for the `str` and `wide` variants through `coded`),
  today's host CSR and the rebuilt host CSR. A path that wins no regime goes, with rule 7 on the regimes it
  served (the cast variants included).
- S1's arms are SON's GPU passes; SON on the CPU stays as the CPU route's streaming strategy whatever S1
  says (rule 9). S2 compares today's multi-GPU waves with the ported skeleton row-split over both GPUs.
- C4's regimes are the batches; a whole path wins a batch by rule 2 on its call time. `count_itemsets_cuda`
  stays for SON whatever C4 says.
- H's "regime where the role runs" is each dumped call's source (dataset, level); rule 10's 10% is on the
  median per call.
- Planted motifs (deep_k, smoke) are checked as the CLAUDE.md policy says (mined ≥ planted), and only
  those planted at or above the workload's min_count: deepk15 (0.15) is above every deep_k motif's
  support, so it checks none and rests on its signature, equal across arms.
- The census is a harness check and is not budgeted. Everything else Phase 2b runs counts against the
  GPU-hours (the H dumps and R5's timing at one device each; R2 and R4 time host code but run in 2b).
